# BUFF — The Founder Story

> Core emotional engine = "the nagging loop." Lead with WHY/WHAT, not mechanics.

I'm Adi — a product manager, and a developer before that. But BUFF didn't start as a product. It started at my kitchen table, with my 15-year-old son, Itay, and a problem neither of us could solve.

We were stuck in a loop. *Did you do your homework? Did you study for the test? Did you text your friend back?* To him it felt like I was micromanaging every inch of his life — exactly what a teenager can't stand. To me it felt like the only alternative was letting go… and every time I let go, things fell apart. He'd forget to hand in assignments. He couldn't break "study for the exam" into something he could actually do day by day. He'd walk in unprepared and come home with a failing grade. Then he'd be crushed about it and come to me for help — and I'd be furious at myself for backing off and letting him fall, when I'd known all along it was too big to carry alone.

That was the loop: nag and resent each other, or let go and watch it collapse. Neither of us wanted to live there.

There was another layer I only understood later. Going through Itay's diagnosis, I started recognizing the same wiring in myself. I've never been formally diagnosed — but I stopped reading my own life as a string of personal failures and started actually understanding it. That's also why the *judgment* in our loop cut so deep for both of us: I knew exactly what it felt like from the inside.

Here's the thing about Itay — he loves computers, he loves AI. And building things is literally what I do. Around that time I'd made a little app to run our neighborhood community, and one day it clicked: why not build something for him and me? He never puts his phone down — so what if the nagging could move *into* the phone? Not me hovering, but something he'd actually think was cool, something he wouldn't be embarrassed to open.

So we sat down together — and *together* is the whole point — and designed it. He decided how he wanted his tasks. He decided what they were worth to him and what he wanted to save up for. He picked the name. He picked the colors. A gamer name and a gamer skin mattered to him more than anything, so that's what we built first.

And it worked, fast. He took the wheel on his own tasks. They were right there in front of him, easy to see, and — this part mattered most — the app never judged him the way my nagging did. I finally got a quiet window into what he was nailing and where he was stuck, without the fight. The whole house just got lighter.

And the proof showed up where it counts. Between the first semester and the second, his grades climbed — dramatically. The chemistry he'd been drowning in came back an 83. Not because I chased him harder, but because he was finally running his own system.

I told my friends about it, lit up, and they wanted it too. I had no real way to give it to them — I'd built it for one family, not many. But that was the first signal: if this many people want in, maybe there's something here.

Then my younger daughter, Emi (9), wanted in too. With her it was less about school — her schoolwork was already dialed in — and more about the independence a 9-year-old is growing into: remembering to pack her own bag, grab her lunch, arranging to walk to school with a friend. The nagging was never as dramatic with her, but she wanted her own BUFF, sized to her age and her challenges. She asked for "girl colors," so I added a pastel theme.

And it was Itay who said it: *if this works for me — a kid with complicated ADHD — it'll work for anyone.* That's when I decided to build it for real.

I started with a simple version, mostly for friends and people I'd met in ADHD-parent communities. We reached almost 200 families, fast.

Then a hard season hit and routines broke — and we saw something painful: when life fell out of routine, many of those families drifted, and a lot didn't come back. It was the hardest lesson, and the most important one. Habits that only survive when life is calm aren't strong enough — and that's exactly the problem worth solving. It's why I decided to rebuild BUFF properly, to last.

So that's where we are now. It started as something I wanted for my own kids. It became something for families like ours. That's how BUFF was born — and we're still building it.

If you're a parent stuck in that same loop — I'd really love to hear your version of it. That's who we're building for.
