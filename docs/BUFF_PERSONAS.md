# BUFF — Personas

> פרסונות הורה + ילד עם sub-types, רגשות שצריך לעורר בכל אחת, וערוצי גילוי.
> מסמך הפנייה לכל ad targeting, landing variant, פוסט בפורום, או החלטת copy.

**עודכן:** 11 במאי 2026
**מקור:** BUFF_PRD §4, beta user research (183 משפחות, Israel + international), שיחות עם Itay (15) ו-Emi (9), Adi
**שפה:** הסברים בעברית. ציטוטי פרסונות (איך הם מדברים על עצמם) באנגלית ראשונה. עברית כתרגום שני כשרלוונטי.

---

## למה המסמך הזה קיים

ה-PRD מגדיר "הורה של ילד עם ADHD, גילים 6–18" כלקוח. **זה לא מספיק כדי לכתוב ad.**

הורה-של-ילד-בן-7-שזה-עתה-אובחן ≠ הורה-של-בן-15-שמסרב-לדבר-איתם. השנייה רוצה דברים שונים, חוששת מדברים שונים, נמצאת בקהילות אחרות, מגיבה לטונים אחרים.

המסמך הזה מפרק את ה-PRD ל-9 פרסונות פעולתיות (5 הורים, 4 ילדים). כל אחת היא יעד שיווקי עצמאי שמסביר *מי, מה, איפה, איך*.

## 1. The 9 Personas at a Glance

```
PARENT PERSONAS (the buyer):
P1. The Exhausted Morning Parent     — daily friction acute, any age
P2. The Post-Diagnosis Parent        — recently learned, ages 6–10
P3. The "Tried Everything" Parent    — burned by past failures, ages 8–14
P4. The Teen-Lost-Control Parent     — teen pulled away, ages 13–18
P5. The Coach-Curious Parent         — already exposed to positive discipline

CHILD PERSONAS (the user):
C1. Emi-type      — 6–9, Pastel default (kid picks at age 9+ if mature enough)
C2. Mid-Range     — 10–12, autonomy-curious — picks Pastel or Gamer
C3. Itay-type     — 13–15, control-sensitive — typically picks Gamer
C4. Late Teen     — 16–18, pre-launch — typically picks Gamer
```

### AHA Event per Persona — what triggers the "life has changed" moment

ה-AHA הוא **event דיסקרטי**, לא feeling. ראי [BUFF_BRAND.md §4](BUFF_BRAND.md) להגדרה המלאה. הטבלה למטה: איזה event-ספציפי הכי סביר שיהיה ה-trigger לכל פרסונה הורית. ה-event הזה הוא ה-paywall trigger.

| Persona | Most likely stated pain at onboarding | The AHA event |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** Exhausted Morning | "Morning chaos" | First morning the kid is dressed/ready before parent had to ask |
| **P2** Post-Diagnosis | "General independence" (broader — she's not sure which pain to pick) | 3 unprompted tasks in any category within 7 days |
| **P3** Tried Everything | "Bag / forgetting things" *(she's seen kid succeed at micro-tasks before but never at the hard ones)* | First night the kid packs the bag for tomorrow unprompted |
| **P4** Teen-Lost-Control | "General independence" — operational details have already broken down, she just wants engagement back | First teen-proposed task that the kid completes |
| **P5** Coach-Curious | "Bag / forgetting things" *(the high-EF task that proves the system, on-philosophy)* | First night the bag is packed unprompted OR first Daily Win Bonus she gives |

**Paywall copy template per persona:** see [BUFF_MESSAGING.md §X](BUFF_MESSAGING.md) (forthcoming section — paywall copy by pain category).

**Kid AHA events** (upstream of parent AHA — kid must hit theirs first for parent's to fire):

| Child Persona | Kid's AHA event |
|---|---|
| C1 Emi-type (6–9) | First reward redemption (real-world). "BUFFs work." |
| C2 Mid-Range (10–12) | First child-proposed task approved by parent. Ownership confirmed. |
| C3 Itay-type (13–15) | First "deal" successfully completed in Gamer Mode. Respect confirmed. |
| C4 Late Teen (16–18) | First week without parent intervention at all. Autonomy confirmed. |

## 3. PARENT PERSONAS

### P1. The Exhausted Morning Parent

**Demographics:** 32–45, mom predominantly (לא בלעדית), kid 6–14, two-income household, urban/suburban
**ADHD journey:** מאומת לפחות 6 חודשים. יודעת מה זה ADHD בערך. **שחוקה מהשגרה.**
**Geo priority:** US > UK > Canada > Australia > Israel

**The daily scene:**
> 6:55am. "Get dressed." 7:01am. "Did you brush your teeth?" 7:08am. "Have you put your shoes on?" 7:12am. "WHERE IS YOUR BAG?" 7:18am, car door slams. She takes a breath. She'll do this again at homework time. Then bedtime. Then tomorrow morning.

**Pain quotes (verbatim — כך היא מתארת את עצמה):**
- *"I'm so tired of repeating myself."*
- *"I feel like a drill sergeant in my own house."*
- *"I love my kid but I hate who I become in the morning."*
- *"I just need 30 seconds where no one is yelling."*

**What she currently tries:**
- Sticker charts (works 3 weeks, fails)
- Yelling (works for that day, ruins the relationship)
- Reminders on her own phone (she forgets to remind)
- ADDitude articles she bookmarks but doesn't have time to read
- Maybe Joon for a month, gave up

**Emotional jobs (ordered):**
1. **Calm** — she's burned out, this is her #1 want
2. **Helping** — she wants to be the good parent again
3. **Control** — knowing what's happening without active management

**Trust signals that work:**
- *"Founded by a mom"* — same boat
- Concrete time savings (*"3 minutes per morning"*)
- Real testimonials with specific named pains
- *"No ads, ever"* promise (her phone is already noisy)

**Objections to overcome:**
- *"Will this become another thing I have to manage?"* → Answer: **Built to fade. Phase 4 = kid doesn't need it.**
- *"Won't my kid lose interest like with Joon?"* → Answer: **Real rewards, not virtual coins.**
- *"Do I need to spend an hour setting it up?"* → Answer: **5 minutes. Onboarding is the demo.**

**Discovery channels (ordered by priority):**
1. Facebook ADHD parenting groups (US: *"ADHD Moms Support Group"*, *"ADHD Parenting Support"*; UK: *"ADHD Parenting Tribe UK"*)
2. Instagram ADHD parenting accounts (@adhd.love, @theadhdfemme, @parents.with.adhd.kids)
3. Google: *"ADHD app for kids that actually works"*, *"morning routine ADHD"*
4. Word of mouth from another mom

**Best hook for her:**
> *"How many times did you say 'just get ready' this morning?"*

**HE:** *"כמה פעמים אמרת היום 'תתלבש כבר'?"*

**Hook that does NOT work:**
- *"Empower your child's executive function!"* (clinical, long, not her language)
- *"Transform your morning!"* (wellness-influencer fluff)

**Anti-patterns — מה שמרחיק אותה:**
- Wellness-influencer tone (*"Mama, your magic morning ritual…"*)
- *"Your child is broken, we'll fix him"* framing
- Long-form clinical content with footnotes
- Anything that **adds** work to her plate
- Hebrew copy that translates English mechanically ("הילד שלך לא עצלן" עובד; "אנחנו עוזרים לך לטפל בכל מה שמפריע לילד שלך" — לא עובד)

### P2. The Post-Diagnosis Parent

**Demographics:** 30–42, parent of 6–10 year old who was diagnosed in the last 6–18 months. Often first child with ADHD diagnosis (so: new territory).
**ADHD journey:** **בלבול.** קיבל אבחנה, רופא נתן ריטלין (אולי), אבל לא ברור מה לעשות *יום-יום*. רוצה ללמוד, מפחד לטעות, חושש שהילד "שונה".

**The scene:**
> She just left the psychiatrist's office. Prescription in bag. "Cognitive Behavioral Therapy referral" in another. School pulled her aside last week and used the words "executive function deficit." She opens Reddit at 11pm: *"My 8yo was just diagnosed with ADHD. Where do I even start?"*

**Pain quotes:**
- *"I don't want to medicate my kid but I don't know what else to do."*
- *"I feel like I'm failing him and I haven't even started."*
- *"Everything I read contradicts everything else I read."*
- *"How do I explain this to my parents/in-laws/the school?"*

**What she currently tries:**
- ADDitude magazine subscription
- Dr. Russell Barkley YouTube binge
- Parenting books (Smart but Scattered, Driven to Distraction)
- Asks pediatrician for "an app that helps"
- Maybe joins a Facebook group

**Emotional jobs (ordered):**
1. **Capable** — "I need to feel like I know what I'm doing"
2. **Hope** — "Tell me this gets better"
3. **Helping** — "Tell me I'm helping, not damaging"

**Trust signals that work:**
- "Research-backed" / *"Based on executive function principles"* (כי היא קוראת ADDitude)
- Founder credibility (Adi as a *real* mom + business background)
- Endorsements from clinicians or ADHD coaches
- Free trial / generous free tier (she's wary of more recurring costs after therapist/meds)

**Objections to overcome:**
- *"Is this scientifically valid?"* → Answer: BUFF design draws on Barkley's executive function work, scaffold-that-fades model.
- *"Will it interfere with what the therapist is doing?"* → Answer: BUFF is a complement, not replacement (PRD §6.4).
- *"Will my kid become dependent on an app?"* → **STRONG answer:** "Outgrowing it is the explicit goal. Phase 4 = kid doesn't need it."

**Discovery channels:**
1. Google searches (*"my child was diagnosed with ADHD, now what"*, *"executive function help for kids"*)
2. ADDitude magazine + their app reviews
3. Pediatrician referrals (long-term play, not MVP)
4. Reddit r/ADHD_parenting

**Best hook for her:**
> *"Your kid isn't lazy. Their brain just needs dopamine."*

**HE:** *"הילד שלך לא עצלן. המוח שלו צריך דופמין."*

**Anti-patterns:**
- Gaming-bro tone (*"Level up your kid!"*)
- Pseudo-science (anything about "rewiring the brain")
- Anything that minimizes the diagnosis

### P3. The "Tried Everything" Parent

**Demographics:** 38–50, kid 8–14, multi-year ADHD veteran, often two-child household (one ADHD), exhausted but **still searching**.
**ADHD journey:** ניסתה הכל. Sticker charts. Joon. Reward jars. Threats. Calm parenting. Naturopath. Sleep coach. Has a folder of school 504 plans. **Skeptical but not yet broken.**

**The scene:**
> Third app this year. She types into the App Store search bar: "ADHD kids" — scrolls past Joon (tried it), Goally (too expensive), Savvy Kid (vague), reaches BUFF. Reads the reviews carefully. Looks for the *specific* complaint that broke her last app.

**Pain quotes:**
- *"Nothing sticks for more than 6 weeks."*
- *"My kid is smart enough to game any system you give him."*
- *"I'm not paying $X/month for another app that fails."*
- *"I need something that fits MY kid, not generic ADHD kid."*

**What she currently tries / has tried:**
- 3+ ADHD apps (Joon, possibly Goally, possibly Savvy Kid)
- Multiple sticker chart variations
- Habitica (adult system, didn't translate)
- Watching ADHD TikTok for hacks
- Therapist's behavioral chart (worked 4 weeks)

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Hope** — has to feel like *this might be different*
2. **Capable** — "I'm a smart parent, I can identify the right tool"
3. **Calm** — distant goal, she's stopped expecting easy

**Trust signals that work:**
- **Specificity** — she rejects generic. The doc that lands her is one that **names her exact past failure** ("Tried Joon? Here's why the Doter burns out in 8 weeks…")
- Anti-marketing tone (she smells hype)
- Detailed product differences (she'll read a comparison table)
- Beta user testimonials with **specific kid ages and durations** ("My 11yo has been using BUFF for 9 months…")

**Objections to overcome:**
- *"How is this actually different from Joon?"* → Answer: real-world rewards, age 13+, outgrowing.
- *"Will my kid game it?"* → Answer: BUFFs require parent approval; child can't self-issue.
- *"What if my kid hates it?"* → Answer: Onboarding is co-designed with child (Vibe Check, Buddy Choice).

**Discovery channels:**
1. App Store search (high-intent, comparison shopper)
2. ADHD Reddit (lurks more than posts — reads our replies)
3. Founder content (LinkedIn, Twitter — if Adi posts about *why* BUFF differs)
4. Comparison blog posts (*"Joon vs Goally vs BUFF"*)

**Best hook for her:**
> *"The reward isn't on the screen — it's in their life."*

**HE:** *"הפרס לא נמצא על המסך — הוא בחיים שלהם."*

**Anti-patterns:**
- *"The #1 app for ADHD kids!"* claims (she's seen 6 of those)
- Vague promises (*"Transform your family!"*)
- Gamification jargon she's already heard

### P4. The Teen-Lost-Control Parent

**Demographics:** 40–55, kid 13–18, ADHD diagnosed years ago, **the relationship is strained**.
**ADHD journey:** הילד הצליח עם כל מיני מערכות בילדות. עכשיו הוא בן 14 ולא רוצה לדבר. ההורה מרגיש שאיבד את התקשורת.

**The scene:**
> Saturday afternoon. Asks 14yo *"Did you finish your science project?"* Gets a one-word answer or door-slam. She doesn't know if it's ADHD or "just being a teen." She knows she's lost the leverage she had at 8.

**Pain quotes:**
- *"He won't even talk to me about school anymore."*
- *"Everything is a fight. I can't even ask if he ate."*
- *"I know he's struggling but he won't let me help."*
- *"He's too old for the kid apps and too young to handle this alone."*

**What she currently tries:**
- Backing off (which feels like giving up)
- Bribing (which feels gross)
- Sending dad in (which sometimes works)
- Therapist for the teen (40% of teens refuse)
- Has heard of Tiimo but it's not family-oriented

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Helping** — "I want to support him, not control him"
2. **Hope** — "We can still have a relationship"
3. **Calm** — secondary, she's not at the burnout edge of P1

**Trust signals that work:**
- **Teen co-designer** — Itay (15, ADHD) helped design Gamer Mode (formerly "Teen Mode"). Massive trust.
- Teen-specific UI (no buddy character, dashboard not pet)
- Privacy promise — parent doesn't see *everything*
- Stories from other teens *in their own voice*

**Objections to overcome:**
- *"He'll see it as another control tool."* → Answer: Gamer Mode is opt-in, kid-controlled. Parent doesn't dictate tasks — they negotiate.
- *"Apps haven't worked since age 11."* → Answer: This one was built with a teen.
- *"He won't use it long enough to matter."* → Answer: Co-design means he had buy-in from minute one.

**Discovery channels:**
1. ADDitude teen-specific content
2. Reddit r/ADHD (the teen sub) — teens themselves recommend it
3. Therapist referrals (high-intent)
4. Word of mouth from another teen-stage parent

**Best hook for her:**
> *"Coach mode, not cop mode."*

**HE:** *"Coach, לא שוטרים."*

(זה הניסוח של ה-Landing Hero בעברית הקיים — מתאים מעולה לפרסונה הזו.)

**Anti-patterns:**
- *"Take back control of your teen!"* (this *kills* her — control is the problem, not the solution)
- *"He needs structure!"* (she knows. she's tried.)
- Anything that suggests punishment or accountability mechanics

### P5. The Coach-Curious Parent

**Demographics:** 35–50, kid any age, **already inside the positive-discipline world**.
**ADHD journey:** קוראת ADDitude, מאזינה ל-Janet Lansbury או דומה, יודעת מה זה "co-regulation" ו-"executive function scaffolding". מחפשת **כלי שלא יסתור את הערכים שלה.**

**The scene:**
> Sees a Joon ad. Closes it immediately — *"video game that makes routines fun"* feels like behaviorism in disguise. Reads BUFF's tagline *"Until they don't need us"* and pauses. Reads the philosophy page. Bookmarks.

**Pain quotes:**
- *"Most parenting apps undo what I'm trying to teach."*
- *"I want a tool that respects my kid as a stakeholder."*
- *"I'll pay more for the right tool."*

**What she currently tries:**
- DIY systems based on Barkley/Greene
- Calendar systems for the family
- Verbal coaching protocols she read in a book

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Capable** — "I'm doing this right"
2. **Helping** — alignment between tools and values
3. **Control** — knowing the tool is on-philosophy

**Trust signals that work:**
- Explicit pillar language (*"Independence over dependency"*, *"70% is success"*)
- Anti-engagement positioning (*"You'll outgrow this app"*)
- Founder transparency about what BUFF is NOT (PRD §6.4)
- Open documentation (this whole `docs/` folder is a brand asset for her — if she ever sees it, she's sold)

**Objections to overcome:**
- *"Is this just behaviorism with better marketing?"* → Answer: real rewards = intrinsic. Streaks removed. No sad buddy. Each Values Check passes Pillar 1.
- *"Will my kid become dependent?"* → **STRONG answer:** ה-Mission tagline.

**Discovery channels:**
1. ADHD coach recommendations (highest-trust channel)
2. Substack writers in ADHD/parenting space
3. Podcast appearances (Adi as guest)
4. Long-form blog content

**Best hook for her:**
> *"Built so they can leave it behind."*

**HE:** *"בנוי כדי שיוכלו לעזוב אותו."*

**Anti-patterns:**
- Anything that sounds like behaviorism (*"reinforce the desired behavior"*)
- Bro-marketing
- Hyperbolic claims

## 4. CHILD PERSONAS

### C1. Emi-type (6–9)

**Reference persona:** Emi, Adi's 9yo daughter (future Pastel Mode user).
**Cognitive stage:** Concrete operations. Visual > verbal. Immediate > delayed.
**ADHD experience:** May or may not be diagnosed yet. Knows they "get distracted" or "have a hard time."

**Daily ADHD scene:**
> Mom asks "Get dressed." She walks to the bedroom, sees a Lego, sits down to play. Mom comes in 5 minutes later: *"Why aren't you dressed?"* Emi looks up: *"I forgot."* She didn't lie. She actually forgot.

**What she feels:**
- Confused about why people are mad at her
- Loves immediate visual feedback (animation, sound, color)
- Easily delighted by small celebrations
- Struggles with "later" — needs "now"

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Success** — "I did it!" (she needs this *loudly* and *often*)
2. **Belonging** — "I'm not weird"
3. **Ownership** — secondary, still developing

**What works in the product:**
- Visual buddy character (BUDDY V0)
- Instant feedback on task completion (sound + animation)
- Short tasks (3 minutes max)
- 70% goal = "I usually win"
- Real reward she chose (toy, movie, day trip)

**What alienates her:**
- Reading-heavy interfaces (she's still building reading speed)
- Long onboarding
- Failure / "missed" messaging
- Streaks (when she misses, the broken streak is a tantrum trigger)

**Brand voice for her (in the app):**
- *"Nice work!"* not *"You completed a task."*
- *"Wanna try this one?"* not *"Your next required task is:"*
- *"BUDDY's been waiting for you!"* not *"Please log in."*

**Hebrew equivalents:**
- *"כל הכבוד!"* / *"רוצה לנסות את זה?"* / *"BUDDY חיכה לך!"*

**Trust signals (from her POV):**
- BUDDY is friendly, never disappointed
- Parent gets notified of *progress*, not *failures*
- She gets to choose her own avatar / BUDDY skin

### C2. Mid-Range (10–12)

**Cognitive stage:** Late concrete, early formal operations. Starts caring about *fairness*. Starts wanting *autonomy*.
**ADHD experience:** Aware of diagnosis. May feel stigmatized at school. Starting to be self-critical.

**Daily scene:**
> Class assigns a 2-week project. He understands the deadline. He cannot start. Three days before due date, panic. Submits half. Teacher comment: *"You can do better."* He internalizes: *"Apparently I can't."*

**What he/she feels:**
- Frustrated by the gap between *knowing what to do* and *doing it*
- Wants to be treated as older than they are
- Starting to resent buddy characters / "kid stuff"
- Wants to choose

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Ownership** — "I decided this"
2. **Success** — "I'm not failing"
3. **Belonging** — "I'm normal"

**What works in the product:**
- Child-proposed tasks (PRD §1.1 feature)
- Child-proposed rewards
- BUDDY presence is optional / customizable
- Visual progress *and* numerical progress (BUFFs balance)
- Earning toward something specific they chose

**What alienates them:**
- Being talked down to
- Babyish color schemes / illustrations
- Parent able to *see everything* (privacy matters more here)
- "You've been doing great!" empty praise

**Brand voice for them:**
- *"Your call."* not *"This is recommended."*
- *"You're 2 away from [reward]."* — specific, not vague
- *"BUDDY noticed you crushed Tuesday."* — observational, not evaluative

**Trust signals:**
- They can edit their reward list
- They see what data the parent sees (transparency)
- Their input affects what tasks appear

### C3. Itay-type (13–15)

**Reference persona:** Itay, Adi's 15yo son, co-designer of Teen UI, has ADHD.
**Cognitive stage:** Formal operations. Hyper-aware of social signaling. Allergic to anything *"for kids."*
**ADHD experience:** Diagnosed for years. Knows the language. Has opinions about meds.

**Daily scene:**
> Wakes up late again. Mom in the doorway: *"Itay, you missed the bus."* He has 3 ADHD apps on his phone — none opened in 4 months. They were "for kids." Tonight he has a deal with mom: if he packs his bag tonight he can have an extra hour of gaming Saturday. He likes the deal. He just needs to remember to pack the bag.

**What he feels:**
- Resents being micromanaged
- Trusts adults who treat him as one
- Wants control over his interface
- Suspicious of buddies, gamification, "fun" framing
- Open to dark mode, minimalism, dashboard interfaces

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Ownership** — "this is mine, parent doesn't dictate"
2. **Belonging** — "I'm just a teen, not a sick teen"
3. **Success** — secondary; he's already started identifying himself as "not great at this"

**What works in the product:**
- No buddy character (per Itay's actual design feedback)
- Dashboard UI (Stitch designs 01–06)
- Dark theme + neon green = "looks like a real app"
- Deal-making interface (he proposes terms with parent)
- BUFFs as currency (gaming-native)
- Streak displayed but not weaponized

**What alienates him:**
- Cute animations
- "Great job!" / praise without substance
- Parent-side dashboards he can see
- "Your parent assigned you:"
- Anything that looks like Joon

**Brand voice for him:**
- *"You set this. You change it."*
- *"3 done. 2 left. Your call."*
- *"Streak: 4 days."* (factual, not celebratory)
- No emojis in copy except neutral ones (→, 💪 borderline OK)

**Trust signals:**
- App actually looks teen-targeted (Stitch designs validate this)
- Privacy from parent (what he marks "personal" stays personal)
- He can turn BUDDY off
- The app doesn't sound like it was written by a parent

### C4. Late Teen (16–18)

**Cognitive stage:** Adult-level reasoning. Identity coalescing. Pre-launch (driver's license, job, college).
**ADHD experience:** Veteran. May be self-medicating with caffeine/cannabis. Often has rejection sensitivity. May have given up on parent-coordinated systems.

**Daily scene:**
> 17, junior year, varsity practice 3x/week, applying to college, working a part-time job. He missed his SAT registration deadline. He has executive dysfunction *and* genuine adult-sized responsibilities. He doesn't need his mom telling him to brush his teeth — he needs a system for *his life*.

**What they feel:**
- Aware of the adult world coming
- Anxious about whether they'll "make it" on their own
- Embarrassed when parents intervene
- Open to *tools*, closed to *parents*

**Emotional jobs:**
1. **Ownership** — "this is my life now"
2. **Success** — practical, life-skill wins
3. **Belonging** — "I'm normal-enough to function as an adult"

**What works in the product:**
- Solo-user mode (parent invisible by default)
- Real-world rewards that map to adult life (gas money, concert ticket, gym membership)
- BUFFs balance shown in real money equivalents
- Integration with calendar (future)
- Pause Mode for exam weeks / breakups / life disruption

**What alienates them:**
- Parent dashboard visible to them
- "Family plan" framing if they're nearly out of the house
- Anything that infantilizes

**Brand voice for them:**
- *"You're 2 weeks from [thing you saved for]."*
- *"Pause if you need to. Come back when you can."*
- No buddy. No skin. Just dashboard.

**Trust signals:**
- App treats them as a near-adult
- Can transfer ownership at 18 (parent off the account)
- Has a path to "graduating" — feature complete, can leave with skills

## 5. Cross-Cutting Notes

### The Buying Parent vs. The Using Child

זה ה-fundamental tension של BUFF marketing:
- **Buyer = Parent**
- **User = Child**

Most ads are aimed at the parent (because they decide & pay). But the *retention* depends on the child. If we acquire parents with copy that promises *"finally control your child"* — the kid will hate the product on day 1.

**כלל ברזל:** כל ad לפרסונה הורית חייב לעבור acid test: "האם הילד שלי, אם יראה את ה-ad הזה, ירגיש מובן או מובס?"

### The Israeli vs. International Split

- **96% of beta users are Israeli** (PRD §4.3)
- **Primary growth market = US/UK/AU**
- **Hebrew copy must be authentic, not translated** — חלק מההורים הישראליות יזהו תרגום מאנגלית ויעצרו (במיוחד P5 — Coach-Curious)
- **English copy must be culturally native** — US Facebook moms speak differently than UK Mumsnet moms

### Pastel Mode vs. Gamer Mode

- C1 (Emi-type, 6–9) → Pastel default; kid picks at age 9 onward
- C2 (Mid-Range, 10–12) → kid picks Pastel or Gamer (no forced age gate)
- C3 (Itay-type, 13–15) → kid picks (typically Gamer per Itay's actual feedback)
- C4 (Late Teen, 16–18) → kid picks (typically Gamer)
- **No forced 12→13 transition.** The kid migrates aesthetic when *they're* ready. Per D-2026-05-11-01 — age is a default heuristic, not a value gate.
