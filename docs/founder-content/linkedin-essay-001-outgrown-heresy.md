## The full draft

> *Title:* **Why I'm building BUFF to be outgrown — a senior PM's heresy**

For a decade as a senior PM, I optimized for one thing: making products users come back to.

DAU. Retention curves. Time-in-app. Habit loops. I built features that compounded engagement and called that good work.

Then I had to build something for my own son.

He has ADHD. By 7, the morning routine was a war zone. By 10, I'd tried every app: sticker charts, Joon, screen-time timers, gamified chore lists. They all worked — for about six weeks. Then his ADHD brain saw through them, the novelty died, and we were back to me repeating "did you brush your teeth?" five times before school.

I looked at what these apps measured and what they optimized for, and I realized something that should have been obvious sooner:

**ADHD apps that win on engagement lose on outcome.**

The kid graduates from a virtual pet to executive function, or to nothing.

---

So I started building BUFF. And almost every PM instinct I'd developed over a decade led me in the wrong direction.

The first version I sketched looked like every other behavior app: streaks, points, level-ups, a buddy character that "grew" when the kid completed tasks. Tight habit loops. The kind of thing that A/B tests beautifully because it spikes 30-day retention.

I ran it past my son.

"What happens if I miss a day?"
"You lose your streak."
"So I have to do this every day or I lose what I built?"
"Yeah."
"That's stressful. I don't want that."

He was 12. He saw the dark pattern in 30 seconds. I, the credentialed product manager, had built it on autopilot.

I threw the prototype out and started over.

---

**The heresy:**

Most senior PMs would never say this in a board meeting. I'm going to say it now, in public, because if I don't claim it publicly I'll be tempted to walk it back when the metrics-pressure starts:

**I want my users to outgrow my product.**

BUFF has explicit phases:

- Phase 1: parent + app remind the kid
- Phase 2: app reminds, parent backs off
- Phase 3: kid self-initiates
- Phase 4: kid doesn't need BUFF anymore

If a kid is still using BUFF at 18, we failed.

If a kid stopped at 14 because they'd built the executive function to run their own routine — we won.

This is why Joon's model (the dominant player in the space) can't bridge to teens, no matter how many features they ship. Their entire engine — Doter pet, coins, in-game rewards — depends on the kid still being IN the system. Letting them go means giving up DAU. Their growth model and their user's actual best interest are in direct conflict.

---

**What's interesting from a PM craft perspective:**

Building for outgrowing requires inverting almost every convention I learned.

- Don't optimize for time-in-app. Optimize for **time-not-needing-app.**
- Don't reward streaks. Reward **70% completion** — because demanding perfection paralyzes ADHD kids before they start.
- Don't gate features behind levels. **Real-life rewards** unlock real-life experiences the kid already wants.
- Don't make the buddy character "need" the kid. The buddy gives, never demands. There is no sad-buddy state, ever.
- Don't measure success in engagement. Measure it in autonomy markers.

When you strip out all the engagement scaffolding most behavior apps depend on, you have to ask: *what's actually making this work?*

The answer turns out to be the same thing that makes a good therapist work.

**The kid is the client.**
**The parent is the coach.**
**The app is the scaffolding.**
**And scaffolding is supposed to fade.**

---

We're launching BUFF in Q3 2026. Two interfaces — Pastel for younger kids, Gamer for teens (co-designed by my 15-year-old, who has ADHD and is brutally honest about which apps feel "babyish"). Real-world rewards the kid chooses themselves. Pause Mode for the weeks when life disrupts the routine. 70%-is-success math everywhere.

And if you've ever quietly thought "my engagement metrics are making the world worse" — let's talk. There's a whole product category to build for the outgrow-as-success thesis. I think this is the start of one.
