## Option 2 — the post (no promotion)

Did you do your homework? Did you study? Did you text your friend back?

I asked my teenager those three questions every single day. To him it felt like I was running his whole life. To me it felt like the only option, because every time I stepped back, things collapsed: assignments not handed in, a test with no prep, a failing grade. Then he'd come to me crushed, and I'd be angry at myself for letting go.

What finally broke the loop wasn't a better way to remind him. It was taking myself out of the reminder seat.

Three things made the difference for us:

1. **He decided.** He chose how his tasks looked, what each one was worth, and what he was saving up for. When it was his system, I stopped being the enemy.
2. **No judgment in the system.** A missed task was just a missed task. No lecture attached, no streak to lose.
3. **I got a quiet window instead of a fight.** I could see where he was stuck without asking "did you...?" ten times.

It's not a miracle. Some weeks fall apart and we start again. But that was the first time he led and I followed.

I'm curious what the loop looks like in your house. What's the sentence you can't stand hearing yourself say in the morning?
