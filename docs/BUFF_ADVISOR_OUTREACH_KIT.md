## 4. Personalization Template

Pitch structure for any new target. Used to draft §5.

### Subject line — disarm the ask
The subject lines that convert in cold-outreach research **explicitly disclaim the commercial ask**. Examples:
- *"Pitching a podcast topic — not a sponsorship"*
- *"For your honest take, not your endorsement"*
- *"An app I think you'd recognize the philosophy of"*

### Body structure (~250 words)

1. **Founder credentials** (1 sentence) — senior PM + mentor + mom of teen with ADHD. Authority + authenticity, both load-bearing per [PERSONAS §P5 trust signals](BUFF_PERSONAS.md).
2. **Specific reference** to a piece of their work — named episode/article/chapter. Not flattery — substantive engagement with their actual idea.
3. **The hinge** — your engagement with their idea + how it shaped BUFF.
4. **2 BUFF design choices** that map to their framework. Concrete, not generic.
5. **The "outgrow" moment** — the unusual third thing. Most ADHD apps optimize against this; BUFF optimizes for it. Differentiator.
6. **Philosophy page link** — `buffadhd.com/philosophy`. Pre-launch-friendly: lets them assess values before installing.
7. **Small ask + opt-out path** — "20 min, no commitment. If after seeing it you'd want X, we talk then. If you'd rather pass, that's also useful."
8. **Honest closing** — acknowledge the work. Avoid sycophancy. Only claim what's true.

### Anti-patterns
- ❌ Generic "love your work" — survives no spam filter
- ❌ Quoting their book without having read it — recipient catches this in reply
- ❌ Asking for an endorsement upfront — kills 90% of replies
- ❌ Multiple links / attachments in cold email — increases spam score
- ❌ Sending from `marketing@` or `noreply@` — destroys founder-voice signal

### 5.1 — Brendan Mahan

**Anchor:** "Wall of Awful" concept + Hachette/Balance book deal *"Overcoming The Wall of Awful: Why It's Hard to Get Things Done When You Have ADHD, Autism, and Anxiety"* + ADHD Essentials podcast back from hiatus.

**Subject (pick one):**
- *"Pitching a podcast topic — not a sponsorship"*
- *"An app shaped around the Wall of Awful"*

**Body:**

```
Hi Brendan,

I'm Adi Elgarat German — senior PM, mentor, and mom of
a 15-year-old with ADHD. I want to pitch you a topic
for ADHD Essentials, not a sponsorship.

The Wall of Awful has shaped how I think about my own
kid for years. What stuck most: the wall isn't built
by the task — it's built by the *accumulation* of
failed attempts at it. Which means any system that
adds another brick (broken streaks, "you missed X
yesterday" counters) makes the wall taller, not
shorter.

That insight became a design constraint for an ADHD
app I'm building called BUFF (ages 6–18). Two of its
core mechanics are essentially the inverse of brick-
laying: a "70% = success" rule that makes 7/10 a win
and never a loss, and a Pause Mode that greets
returning kids with "Welcome back" instead of a
missed-days count. The unusual part — and why I think
there's an episode in it — is that the explicit
success metric is a kid who *no longer needs the
app*. Most ADHD apps optimize against that. We
optimize for it.

Possible angles for an episode:
- "Designing tools that don't build walls" — product
  design through your framework
- "What a senior PM noticed about ADHD apps when she
  became a mom of one"
- "Build to be outgrown — the engagement-metric heresy"

Congrats on the Hachette deal, by the way. The
framework deserves the wider stage.

20 min on a call to test whether any of this lands?
Either way, thank you for the work.

— Adi
linkedin.com/in/adi-elgarat-german
```

**Why it works:** Pitches him as podcast host first (his easiest "yes"), not as advisor. Engages substantively with his own framework. Three pre-baked episode angles reduce his cognitive cost of saying "yes."

— Adi
linkedin.com/in/adi-elgarat-german
