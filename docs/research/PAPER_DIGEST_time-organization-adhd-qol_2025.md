## 0. TL;DR — the one sentence

> **In adults with ADHD, how badly you *feel* after a disorganized day predicts your quality of life more than how well (or how fast) you actually organized it.**

That is Pillar 2 (Positive Coaching) with a p-value. Everything else in this digest hangs off it.

Second sentence, for Teen Mode: **the executive-function profile flips between childhood and adulthood** — kids struggle most with behavioral regulation, adults with metacognition (planning, monitoring, initiating). BUFF's scaffold-that-fades should fade *into* metacognition support, not just fewer reminders.

## 1. What the study is (and is not) — read before quoting anything

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | 69 adults with a formal ADHD diagnosis **plus** at least one EF deficit on BRIEF-A, vs 52 matched neurotypical controls. Israel, ages 21–46, employed ≥3 months, Hebrew readers. 65–69% women. 17 of the 69 also had a learning disability; 8 had a psychiatric comorbidity. |
| **What they measured** | Self-report only: TOPS (time organization: pace / performance / emotional response), BRIEF-A (executive functions: metacognition index + behavioral regulation index), AAQoL (ADHD quality of life: productivity / psychological health / relationships / life outlook). |
| **Design** | Cross-sectional, associative. Data collected 2017–2018. The authors say explicitly: *"the regression models presented here are associative and explanatory… rather than causal or temporal prediction."* (§3.5, §4.4) |
| **What it is NOT** | Not about children. Not about any app or intervention. Not causal. Not a clinical trial. |

**Hard rule for every asset derived from this paper:** say "a 2025 study of adults with ADHD" — never imply it studied kids, never imply BUFF was tested, never use "proven." P2 and P5 personas read ADDitude and check sources; misquoting one Israeli study costs more trust than it buys. This is the same distinction FAQ Q28 already draws ("principles are research-supported, BUFF is not clinically tested").

## 2. The findings, ranked by usefulness to BUFF

### F1. The emotional aftermath of disorganization is the strongest time-related predictor of quality of life
- TOPS Domain C ("frequency of emotional responses following disorganization in time") explained **9%** of QoL variance beyond diagnosis. Actual day-organization performance (Domain B) added only **1.8%**. Pace (Domain A, "doing tasks at an appropriate speed") **did not predict QoL at all.** (§3.5, Table 3)
- Emotional responses correlated with the *behavioral regulation* side of EF (r = −0.50), while performance correlated with *metacognition* (r = −0.55 to −0.58). (§3.4)
- The authors interpret via Barkley: weak emotional control → "frequent emotional reactions following disorganization in time." (§4.3)

**What it means for BUFF:** the moment that matters is not the late morning; it is the twenty minutes after it. BUFF's design bets ("no counts of failure", Welcome Back, Pause without guilt, "~3 tasks = a good day", neutral Yesterday Recap) all target exactly this mechanism. We now have a citable reason.

### F2. Speed is not the problem. Slow is fine.
- Adults with ADHD *did* report a slower pace (3.24 vs 4.46, η² = 0.41), but pace had zero unique contribution to QoL once emotion and metacognition were in the model. (Table 1, Table 3)

**What it means for BUFF:** an evidence anchor for the Spaceship Test (BRAND §7.7): no countdown timers, no "hurry" mechanics, no red clocks. The thing to optimize is "did the day work out and how did it feel", not "how fast".

### F3. Metacognition is the executive function that matters most, and it dominates in adults (not in kids)
- Metacognition Index explained **15.1%** of QoL beyond diagnosis — more than all time-organization domains combined (10.8%). (Abstract, §3.5)
- ADHD adults' deficit was larger in metacognition (M = 75.15) than behavioral regulation (M = 67.94). The authors note this "may differ from the greater behavioral-regulation difficulties observed in children with ADHD." (§3.2, §4.1)
- Metacognition per BRIEF-A = initiate, working memory, plan/organize, task monitor, organization of materials.

**What it means for BUFF:** the 6→18 arc is a shift from regulating behavior (Pastel Mode: buddy, reminders, one task at a time) to regulating *thinking* (Gamer Mode: plan tomorrow, monitor yourself, initiate). This is a sharper story for "the app you outgrow" than "fewer reminders over time".

### F4. "Life productivity" is where ADHD adults feel it most
- Productivity: 52 vs 85 (η² = 0.65) — the largest gap of the four QoL domains, bigger than psychological health (0.35), relationships (0.36), outlook (0.25). Total QoL Cohen's d ≈ 2.17. (Table 2)
- The authors contrast this with children, where prior research found QoL hits mainly in *social* domains (family, peers). (§4.2)

**What it means for BUFF:** for the *kid*, the felt cost of ADHD is relational (fights with parents, peers) — which is what BUFF's "from battles to wins" targets. For the *adult they become*, it is "getting things done". The mission tagline bridges the two.

### F5. Organizing "a whole day satisfactorily" is the biggest performance gap
- TOPS Domain B (success organizing a whole day / period) had the largest between-group effect of the three time domains: 2.53 vs 4.15, η² = 0.55. (Table 1)

**What it means for BUFF:** "was today a good day" is the right unit, not "was each task on time". The count rule (min(3, assigned) = a win, D-2026-06-14) is a Domain-B proxy that is achievable on purpose.

### F6. Supporting citations the paper leans on (not its own findings, cite as such)
- Adults with ADHD "prefer small, immediate rewards over larger, delayed rewards" (ref 25) → "always close to a win" reinforcement pacing (PRD §6.3).
- Weiss (ref 63): "rather than mere symptom reduction, functional improvement and QoL should be regarded as primary outcomes." → BUFF measuring autonomy markers, not symptom scores (PRD §10.1).
- ADHD is "underdiagnosed and undertreated in adults, leading to chronicity" (refs 2, 3); DSM-5-TR adult prevalence 2.5%. → The stakes of childhood scaffolding are adult QoL.
- Time organization needed "to work independently, complete long-term projects, and maintain consistent daily routines" (refs 11, 39).

### F7. A domain the paper measured but didn't analyze: routine disruption
- TOPS Domain D = "the influence of change in routines and various stimuli on the individual's organization of time" — kept for clinical use, not analyzed. (§2.3.2)

**What it means for BUFF:** the instrument itself treats routine disruption as its own clinical dimension. Pause Mode, Welcome Back and the Off-Routine card sit exactly there. No number to quote, but it validates that "disruption" is a recognized construct, not a BUFF-invented one.

## 4. Product — features to sharpen (as interview hypotheses, not builds)

Development is frozen until the ~2026-10-02 decision gate. Each item below is phrased as (a) what the paper suggests, (b) what BUFF already has, (c) the interview question that would confirm or kill it.

### 4.1 The after-moment (Domain C) is under-instrumented
- **Paper:** the emotional response *after* disorganization is the lever.
- **BUFF today:** Vibe Check is at the *start* of the day (energy 1–5, private to the kid, SOS optional). BUDDY end-of-day exists on the kid side; Yesterday Recap is parent-side and read-only. There is no kid-side "how did today feel" moment, and no parent-side guidance for the after-moment.
- **Hypothesis:** a one-tap, kid-only, never-shared end-of-day feel check (same battery/face UI, no wrong answer) plus the BUDDY reacting neutrally-warm regardless of the count. Values risk: must never become a mood log the parent reads as a failure signal (PRD §6.4, FAQ Q23 privacy).
- **Ask in Track B (retained) and Track C (strangers):** *"What happens in your house in the ten minutes after a bad morning?"* and *"Does your kid ever bring up a bad morning again later that day?"*

### 4.2 Teen Mode = metacognition scaffold, say it out loud
- **Paper:** adults' dominant deficit is planning / monitoring / initiation.
- **BUFF today:** Gamer Mode already has the pieces: tomorrow pack (plan), My Stats (monitor), deal-making and child-proposed tasks (initiate). They are not framed as one arc.
- **Hypothesis:** the scaffold phases (PRD §6.1) could be described as *reminders fade, planning prompts grow*: Phase 2 the app reminds; Phase 3 the app asks "what's your plan for tomorrow?" instead of reminding. No new screens; a copy and sequencing change, post-freeze.
- **Ask in Track B/C (teen families):** *"Does your teen plan tomorrow, or react to it?"* / *"If the app stopped reminding and instead asked them to plan, would they?"*

### 4.3 Keep "good day" as the unit; keep speed out
- **Paper:** Domain B (satisfactory whole day) matters; pace does not.
- **BUFF today:** count rule (min(3, assigned)) is exactly this. Task library has two "ADDitude-Pomodoro" starter tasks (`starterTasks/taskLibrary.ts:311,317`) — chunking, not countdowns; fine, but worth a copy check that they never read as "finish in X minutes".
- **No action.** Evidence anchor for D-2026-06-14 and BRAND §7.7 if either is ever challenged.

### 4.4 Parent dashboard metric that matters
- **Paper:** for QoL, "how the day ended" beats "% complete".
- **BUFF today:** the parent sees completions, BUFFs, Vibe trends (energy). Yesterday Recap is neutral.
- **Hypothesis:** the most useful parent-side signal may not be a completion percentage at all but "days that ended calm" — which BUFF cannot measure without 4.1. Park until 4.1 is validated.

### 4.5 Routine disruption is a recognized clinical domain
- **Paper:** TOPS Domain D exists for clinical use.
- **BUFF today:** Pause Mode, Welcome Back, Off-Routine card, no-school-day gap (F-2026-05-21-01).
- **No action;** cite when explaining Pause Mode to clinicians (§5.2).

## 5. A different approach — parents, kids, and one new channel

### 5.1 Parents: move the coaching target from 7:18 to 7:38
BUFF's copy is built around the chaotic morning (H1, H3, P1 scene). The paper says the morning is not where the long-term damage is; the aftermath is. That suggests a second layer of parent-facing content and product tone: *what you do after the failed routine* (the car ride, the evening after homework didn't happen). "Coach mode, not cop mode" gets a mechanism: the cop replays the failure; the coach makes the aftermath boring. This is an extrapolation from adult self-report to parent behavior; label it as BUFF's reading, not the study's finding.

### 5.2 Kids and teens: from "be on time" to "did your day work?"
Copy shift, Values-consistent: never "you were late", always "was today okay?" For C3/C4, the metacognition angle ("you plan it, you check it, your call") matches Itay's own brand voice lines (PERSONAS C3: *"You set this. You change it."*).

### 5.3 The ADHD parent
The study population (adults 21–46) is the parent generation. ADHD is highly heritable; a meaningful share of P1 mothers are themselves the study's subjects. Content that says "if mornings are chaos for you too" speaks to them without a new persona. Tracy Otsuka's audience (ADVISOR_OUTREACH_KIT #10, mothers-with-ADHD) is the existing channel for it.

### 5.4 New channel: occupational therapists (מרפאות בעיסוק)
The paper comes from an OT department, and TOPS is an OT instrument. In Israel, OTs are a primary professional for ADHD kids' daily-function work (time organization, bag packing, morning routine — literally BUFF's task list), and they recommend tools to parents. They are absent from ADVISOR_OUTREACH_KIT (which lists coaches, psychiatrists, podcasters). Suggest: add OTs as channel G in the kit; a Hebrew one-pager "BUFF for OT practice" would be cheap. Note the standing constraint D-2026-05-02-29 (Cog-Fun / Maeir is off-limits without a formal clinician relationship) — Rosenblum's lab is a different group, but the same caution applies: feedback request, not endorsement.
